## What it shows
- **One word broke it.** All 9 uncovered picks are three questions, each asked three times, each wrong all three
  times: q03 (count), q06 (count) and q12 (lookup). All three say "area" for `platform`. The pick chose `kind`
  (q03, three times the same pick), `path` or `status` alone instead, and on q06 twice named a column that does not
  exist (which name was not kept). The fourth question that needs `platform`, q18 ("in the "Phone" area"), was
  covered 3 of 3; the quoted value is the likely cue, not tested.
- **Why a names-only pick can fail here.** Call 1 sees 16 column names and no values. "Money" is a value of
  `platform`, but nothing in the names says so, and "area" sits as close to `kind` or `path` as to `platform`. The whole
  file settles it at once (`toml` answered q03, q06 and q12 correctly in all three passes), which is the reading
  the pick gives up in exchange for its savings.
- **Everything else mapped.** Eleven other columns were needed (`status`, `kind`, `sweep`, `path`, `check`, `stop`,
  `note`, `readme`, `merge_policy`, `remote`, `due`), through "cleanup job", "give-up condition", "documentation
  template", "lifecycle state", "deadline", "hosted somewhere other than" and the like: 63 of 72 needed fields
  found, and the nine missing are all `platform`. The README guessed `readme`, `check` and `stop` would be the weak
  spots; they were covered every time.
- **Reading was fine when the pick was.** Where the pick covered the need, 47 of 51 answers were right; where it did
  not, 0 of 9. Extra fields: 14 of 60 picks (23%) added one, `kind` ×5, `platform` ×3, `path` ×2, `stop` ×1, `card` ×1.
- **The gap is 10 answers: 9 are the "area" questions and 1 is a read miss** (q18 once, a covered pick, one repo of
  four left out). q15 ("active" repos with no give-up condition) is wrong in all three views, 9 of 9 (one or two
  repos too many each time); 02's `view` missed it too (one repo too many), so it is a reading problem of that
  question, not of the pick. It costs `toml` and `view` the same 3 answers.
- **Against the prediction (README):** coverage 85% (predicted about 90%), accuracy 78% against `toml`'s 95%
  (predicted 92% and 98%), tokens 27% (predicted 25%).
- **Tokens and time.** `toml` is 159 tokens above 02's run (15,020 against 14,861) and `view` 158 above (2,590
  against 2,432) on the same file, so the fixed per-call overhead rose by about 158 between the runs (inferred; why

## Limits
- "Area" is one word chosen by me. A different everyday word may have passed; this run has one wording per field and
  cannot say. What it does say: an everyday word that a column name does not settle fails every pass, and the
  failure is not random (q03 picked the same wrong fields three times).
- Everyday words are the mild end of vague: each question still maps one concept to one column. An indirect
  question is untested.
- The two calls are separate `claude -p` processes, each paying the fixed overhead, as in 02.
- One file, 20 mechanical questions, n = 60 per view. The stop was hit by 10 answers, 9 of them from three questions
  that share one word, so the margin is real but narrow in what it covers.
